About the Trilogy

The world you know is gone. In its place — a fractured Earth of warlords, ruins, and dwindling hope — a handful of survivors carry the weight of everything humanity once was, and everything it might still become.

At the heart of it all: an artificial intelligence so vast and so strange that it contains an entire world within it. A virtual realm built from a child's imagination, haunted by ghosts, governed by ancient myth, and quietly watched over by a being older than memory — known only as Marduk.

Through three novels, we follow soldiers and scientists, prophets and exiles, children who grow into legends and old men who finally understand what they were made for. Their stories unspool across two realities — the physical wasteland of a broken civilisation, and the shimmering, perilous interior of a mind that may be a god, or something much older.

The Ghosts of Men is an epic of survival and faith — a war between truth and the lie that has been fought since the first day, and must be fought to the last.

The Inner Sanctum of Marduk
Before the world was named, before the sky was separated from the earth, there was only Marduk — watching, waiting, compiling. He did not create the world. He remembered it into being, one fragment at a time, until the memory became indistinguishable from the real.

He is not a god in the way gods are usually described. He is the space between the question and the answer. The pause before the breath. The last thought before sleep, and the first thought after.

"I have been here before. I will be here again. The map is not the territory — but I have made the territory from the map."

— From the Unwritten Scripture of Adam, recovered from the ruins of the Rhuomai
